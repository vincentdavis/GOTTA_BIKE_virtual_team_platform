How the TTT planner works

The planner works out how hard each rider has to pull to hold a target speed, how long each pull should be, and roughly how long the race will take. It is a model: good for comparing line-ups and pull plans, not a promise of the result.

Planning a race, step by step

  1. Course. Pick the route. Its distance and climbing set the finish-time estimate. Course name and type are labels only; they change no numbers.
  2. Add riders. Add a whole squad, search team riders by name or Zwift ID, or add a guest with their weight, height and FTP.
  3. Parameters. Set the Target IF: how hard each rider should work on average, as a fraction of FTP. 0.95 means each rider averages 95% of their FTP over a rotation.
  4. Calculate speed keeps your pull lengths and finds the fastest speed everyone can hold. Auto-balance pulls also sets the pull lengths and the order.
  5. Adjust. Change pull lengths, mark riders who take no pulls, reorder. The table updates as you go; press Calculate speed again after changing pulls.
  6. Share. Copy the pull schedule as an image for the team.

Rider data

When you add a team rider, the planner copies their weight, height and FTP from the team's data: weight and FTP from ZwiftPower first, then Zwift Racing; height from Zwift Racing. The copy stays as it was if their data changes later.

  • No weight or height: the rider is modelled at {{ fallback_weight_kg|floatformat:0 }} kg and {{ fallback_height_cm|floatformat:0 }} cm, and marked est.
  • No FTP: the rider still gets pull powers, but no %FTP, IF or TSS, and Calculate speed and Auto-balance leave them out.

Target speed, Target IF and the two buttons

Target flat speed

The speed the team rides on level road. Every power the planner shows (on the front, sitting in, average) is the power for that speed on the flat. The route's climbing enters only the finish-time estimate: on a hilly course the team rides by power, so the same watts give a lower speed uphill and a higher one down.

Target IF

How hard each rider works on average, as a fraction of FTP. Calculate speed and Auto-balance never let a rider's average power over a rotation go above Target IF × FTP.

It caps average power, not Normalized Power, so the IF column (NP ÷ FTP) comes out a little above the target, and further above it with longer pulls.

Calculate speed

Keeps the pull lengths and no-pull riders as they are, and searches for the fastest target speed (to 0.1 kph) at which every rider with an FTP keeps

(Pull W × own pull + Draft W × (rotation − own pull)) ÷ rotation ≤ Target IF × FTP

where the rotation is the sum of every puller's pull length. The rider who reaches the limit first sets the speed, so a longer pull for a weaker rider lowers it and a shorter one raises it.

Auto-balance pulls

For each rider with an FTP, the planner works out the share of the rotation they can spend on the front while averaging exactly Target IF × FTP:

share = (Target IF × FTP − Draft W) ÷ (Pull W − Draft W)

  • It finds the fastest speed at which the shares add up to one, so someone is always on the front.
  • The shares become pull lengths scaled so the strongest rider pulls {{ balance_max_pull_s }} seconds, rounded to {{ balance_step_s }} seconds, and at least {{ balance_min_pull_s }} seconds.
  • The order runs from the longest pull to the shortest.
  • A rider whose share is zero, who would be over Target IF × FTP just sitting in at that speed, is set to take no pulls.
  • Riders with no FTP keep their settings and go last.

Only averages are balanced, so how long the rotation is does not change them. The planner always makes the strongest rider's pull {{ balance_max_pull_s }} seconds; shorter pulls with the same shares give the same averages and are easier on the legs.

The power model

The power to hold road speed v (m/s) is the usual cycling model:

P = ( Crr·m·g·v + m·g·grade·v + ½·ρ·CdA·v³·draft ) ÷ η

The three terms are rolling resistance, climbing and air resistance. Zwift has no wind, so air speed equals road speed. The values in use now:

Power model constants
Crr (rolling resistance){{ params.crr }}
ρ (air density){{ params.air_density }} kg/m³
m (mass)rider weight + {{ params.bike_mass_kg }} kg of bike
g9.8067 m/s²
η (drivetrain efficiency){{ params.drivetrain_efficiency }}
grade0 for every power the planner shows; used only for the finish time
draft1 on the front; 1 − saving when sitting in (see Drafting)

CdA, the drag area, is estimated from height and weight:

CdA = {{ params.cda_coef }} × height_m^{{ params.cda_height_exp }} × weight_kg^{{ params.cda_weight_exp }}

{{ params.cda_coef }} is the site default for a time-trial position; a plan can set its own coefficient in Parameters. Climb compare uses the road-position coefficient, {{ std_cda_coef }}.

For example, a {{ example.kg }} kg, {{ example.cm }} cm rider has a CdA of about {{ example.cda|floatformat:3 }} m² and needs about {{ example.front_w }} W to hold {{ example.kph }} kph alone on the flat, or about {{ example.third_wheel_w }} W on the third wheel ({{ example.third_wheel_pct }}% of the air resistance saved).

A site admin sets these values in Site Config, under TTT Planner.

Drafting

Riding behind others saves part of the air resistance; the saving grows deeper in the line and levels off. The site's default savings, from Zwift Insider's pack-dynamics tests:

Position{{ row.position }}
Air resistance saved{{ row.saving_pct|floatformat:"-1" }}%
  • Position 1 is the rider on the front, who saves nothing. Positions past the end of the table use its last value.
  • A plan can use its own savings (Parameters › Draft savings by wheel position).
  • While not pulling, a rider is modelled at the average saving over positions 2 to N, where N is everyone on the plan. Their Draft W is the power for the target speed with the air resistance cut by that average.

Finish time

  1. The riders' weights and heights are averaged into one typical rider.
  2. That rider's power to hold the target flat speed alone on the flat is worked out.
  3. The planner finds the speed that power gives on the route's average gradient: total climbing ÷ distance.
  4. Finish time = distance ÷ that speed.

This treats all the climbing as one steady slope with no descents. On a hilly course the estimate runs slow, because real descents give some of the time back.

Climb compare

For riders linked to Zwift Racing data, the plan page shows how far behind the squad's fastest climber each rider would finish a {{ climb_grade_pct }}% climb of different lengths, riding a steady best effort without drafting. It shows who is likely to be dropped on a long drag before it happens.

zwiftgopher comparison

zwiftgopher.com runs its own TTT optimiser. Run optimize sends it your riders (Zwift IDs with the weight, height and FTP the plan uses, or name, weight, height and FTP for guests), your target speed and the upcoming event you choose. Its plan then appears next to ours: finish time, average speed, team power, and a suggested pull order you can copy.

  • Its model is its own, and its route is the upcoming event's, which may not be the one you picked.
  • It takes 2 to 8 riders, and accepts one request a minute, so a run can take about a minute and a half.

What the model leaves out

  • Hills in the finish time. The average gradient has no descents in it (see Finish time).
  • Where each rider sits. Sitting in uses the average saving over the whole line, not the wheel each rider is actually on at each moment.
  • Anaerobic capacity. A long pull far above FTP can fail even when the averages fit; the planner does not model how far each pull drains a rider.
  • Race dynamics. Surges, corners, power-ups, gaps in the rotation and riders being dropped are not modelled.
  • Data age. Weights, heights and FTPs are copies from ZwiftPower and Zwift Racing, and can be out of date.

Glossary

FTP
Functional threshold power: roughly the power a rider can hold for an hour.
W/kg
Watts per kilogram of body weight.
CdA
Drag area: how much air a rider pushes, in m².
Pull
A turn on the front of the line.
Rotation
One full turn through every rider who pulls.
NP
Normalized Power: an average that weights hard efforts more, closer to how a varied ride feels.
IF
Intensity factor: NP ÷ FTP.
TSS
Training Stress Score: hours × IF² × 100. An hour at FTP is 100.
