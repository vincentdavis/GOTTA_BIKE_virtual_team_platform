How the TTT planner works

The planner works out how hard each rider has to pull to hold a target speed, how long each pull should be, and roughly how long the race will take. It is a model: good for comparing line-ups and pull plans, not a promise of the result.

Planning a race, step by step

  1. Course. Pick the route. Its distance and climbing set the finish-time estimate. Course name and type are labels only; they change no numbers.
  2. Add riders. Add a whole squad, search team riders by name or Zwift ID, or add a guest with their weight, height and FTP.
  3. Parameters. Choose the goal: how fast (as fast as the limits allow, or a speed to hold) and how the pull lengths are set (for even effort, for the most W′ in reserve, or as you set them). Set the limits: the Target IF, the most any rider's IF may reach, and the W′ reserve, the share of W′ every rider keeps in hand; and the shortest and longest pull Optimize may give.
  4. Optimize carries out the goal. The sentence beside the button says what it will do.
  5. Adjust. Change pull lengths, mark riders who take no pulls, reorder. The table and the line above it, which says whether the plan fits the limits, update as you go.
  6. Share. Copy the pull schedule as an image for the team.

Rider data

When you add a team rider, the planner copies their weight, height and FTP from the team's data: weight and FTP from ZwiftPower first, then Zwift Racing; height from Zwift Racing. Their critical power (CP) and W′ come from Zwift Racing. The copy stays as it was if their data changes later.

  • No weight or height: the rider is modelled at {{ fallback_weight_kg|floatformat:0 }} kg and {{ fallback_height_cm|floatformat:0 }} cm, and marked est.
  • No CP or W′ (guests, or riders Zwift Racing has no figures for): the planner uses their FTP as CP and {{ fallback_w_prime_kj }} kJ as W′, close to the team's average, and shows those values with a ~tilde in front.
  • No FTP: the rider still gets pull powers, but no %FTP, IF, TSS or W′ figures, and Optimize cannot judge them: it leaves their pull as it is and puts them last.

The goal, the limits and Optimize

Target flat speed

The speed the team rides on level road. Every power the planner shows (on the front, sitting in, average) is the power for that speed on the flat. The route's climbing enters only the finish-time estimate: on a hilly course the team rides by power, so the same watts give a lower speed uphill and a higher one down.

The two limits

Every rider with an FTP, including riders who take no pulls, is held to two limits:

  • Target IF (a new plan starts at {{ defaults.target_if }}): the rider's IF, Normalized Power ÷ FTP over the race, may not go above it.
  • W′ reserve (starts at {{ defaults.w_prime_reserve_pct }}%): the rider's W′ balance may not drop below that share of their W′ at any point in the race (see W′ balance).

Riders who take no pulls count too, because they still have to hold the wheel: the plan never goes faster by leaving someone to be dropped. If one rider cannot even sit in at the speed the others could hold, that rider sets the speed; the table marks anyone past a limit over target.

Does the plan fit?

The line above the pull schedule answers that for the plan as it stands, whatever produced it. It says whether every rider is inside both limits at the target speed, and if not, who is past which limit. It also gives the fastest speed the current pull lengths allow, to 0.1 kph, and names whoever would cross a limit if the team went any faster: that rider and that limit are what hold the plan back.

The goal

A plan's goal has two halves, chosen in the Parameters card and kept with the plan.

How fast
As fast as the limits allow finds the fastest target speed at which every rider stays inside both limits. Hold keeps the speed you type; typing a speed chooses it.
Pull lengths
Even effort gives stronger riders longer pulls, until everyone's IF is as even as the pull steps allow. Most W′ in reserve keeps pulls short enough that every rider holds on to as much W′ as possible, for surges and the finish. Keep my pull lengths leaves them, and the order, as they are.

Optimize carries the goal out, and the sentence beside the button always says what it will do. As fast as the limits allow with your pull lengths kept is what the planner used to call Calculate speed; with even effort, it is the old Auto-balance. Holding a speed with your pull lengths kept leaves nothing to optimize, so the button is off.

How Optimize sets the pull lengths

  1. When the goal is as fast as the limits allow, it first finds the top speed. It tries a speed: if any rider could not sit in at it within the limits, the speed is too fast. Otherwise it tries rotations from the shortest up, giving every rider the longest pull they can take within the limits, from the shortest pull (a new plan: {{ defaults.min_pull_s }} s) to the longest ({{ defaults.max_pull_s }} s) in steps ({{ defaults.pull_step_s }} s); a rider who cannot manage even the shortest pull sits in. The speed works if some rotation can be filled that way, and halving its way between speeds finds the fastest that works.
  2. At that speed, or at the speed you hold, it tries every rotation length. In each, it finds the pull lengths that keep the hardest-working rider's IF lowest (Even effort), or the lowest W′ balance highest (Most W′ in reserve), handing out the front time a step at a time to whoever it costs least.
  3. The rotation whose worst rider fares best wins; a tie goes to the one whose next-worst rider fares best, and so on, then to the other measure, then to the shorter rotation.
  4. Even effort keeps every rider's W′ above the reserve, and Most W′ in reserve keeps every IF at or under Target IF. If the speed you hold is too fast for that, Optimize says so and sets the pulls anyway, without that limit.
  5. The order runs from the longest pull to the shortest, then the riders who sit in. Riders with no FTP keep their settings and go last.
  6. When the goal is as fast as the limits allow, the speed is then worked out for those pulls exactly as with your pull lengths kept, so optimizing again that way changes nothing.

Pulls are chosen as if the race were long, which is the worst case for W′; the table and the line above it judge the race you planned.

What the pull schedule shows

#, kg, FTP
Pull order, and the rider's data (see Rider data).
CdA
Estimated drag area in m², the rider's body and TT bike together (see The power model).
Pull W
Power to hold the target flat speed on the front, with no draft. A custom pull power set for the rider replaces it.
W/kg
Pull W ÷ weight.
Pull %FTP
Pull W ÷ FTP. Above 100% is normal: riders recover in the draft between pulls.
Avg W
Average power over one rotation: Pull W for the rider's own pull, Draft W for the rest. A rider who takes no pulls averages their Draft W.
NP
Normalized Power over the race: a 30-second rolling average of the rider's power, raised to the fourth power, averaged, and the fourth root taken. Their power repeats every rotation, so the planner works it out exactly for one rotation, which needs no route.
IF
NP ÷ FTP: the usual intensity factor, and what Target IF limits.
TSS
Race hours × IF² × 100. Needs a route, for the race length.
CP, W′ kJ
Critical power and W′ from Zwift Racing; a ~tilde marks a stand-in (see Rider data).
Low W′bal
The least W′ the rider has left at any point in the race, as a share of their W′. Under the W′ reserve it is highlighted; below zero it reads empty: the rider would run out and be dropped.
Pull (s), No pull
The rider's pull length, and whether they sit in the whole race.
Team card
Avg team power is the mean of the riders' Avg W. Est. finish time is explained under Finish time; the speed beside it is the distance raced, lead-in included, over that time.

The power model

The power to hold road speed v (m/s) is the usual cycling model:

P = ( Crr·m·g·v + m·g·grade·v + ½·ρ·CdA·v³·draft ) ÷ η

The three terms are rolling resistance, climbing and air resistance. Zwift has no wind, so air speed equals road speed. The values in use now:

Crr (rolling resistance){{ params.crr }}
ρ (air density){{ params.air_density }} kg/m³
m (mass)rider weight + {{ params.bike_mass_kg }} kg of bike
g9.8067 m/s²
η (drivetrain efficiency){{ params.drivetrain_efficiency }}
grade0 for every power in the pull schedule; the route's own slopes in the finish time and On the course
draft1 on the front; 1 − saving when sitting in (see Drafting)

CdA, the drag area, is the rider's body, estimated from height and weight, plus their bike:

CdA = {{ params.cda_coef }} × height_m^{{ params.cda_height_exp }} × weight_kg^{{ params.cda_weight_exp }} {{ bike_term }}

This is how Zwift itself works: a rider's drag comes from their body, and each piece of equipment adds or takes away a fixed amount. There is no riding position in Zwift; a TT is faster only because of the kit. The body part matches Zwift's own formula, recovered from the game by the Zwift Speed Lab, to within about 1% for ordinary builds.

The planner assumes every rider is on a TT bike: a TT frame with a deep front wheel and a disc, which {% if params.bike_cda_m2 < 0 %}takes {{ bike_area_m2 }} m² off{% else %}adds {{ bike_area_m2 }} m² to{% endif %} the drag area, by the Speed Lab's equipment data. That makes a difference: for a {{ example.kg }} kg, {{ example.cm }} cm rider, holding {{ example.kph }} kph on the flat takes about {{ example.road_w }} W on a road bike and {{ example.front_w }} W on the TT bike. The saving is a fixed area, so it matters more to a small rider. A plan can set its own body coefficient in Parameters (Aero CdA coefficient). Climb compare models upright road riders: no TT bike, and the coefficient {{ std_cda_coef }}.

For example, a {{ example.kg }} kg, {{ example.cm }} cm rider has a CdA of about {{ example.cda|floatformat:3 }} m² and needs about {{ example.front_w }} W to hold {{ example.kph }} kph alone on the flat, or about {{ example.third_wheel_w }} W on the third wheel ({{ example.third_wheel_pct }}% of the air resistance saved).

A site admin sets these values in Site Config, under TTT Planner.

W′ balance

A pull above a rider's critical power (CP) spends a limited store of work, W′ ("W-prime"). Sitting in below CP refills it, faster the further below CP the rider is and the more is missing. W′ balance is what is left. It is what decides whether a hard pull can be repeated, which an average or IF cannot show.

The planner uses the differential W′ balance model (Skiba 2015, after Froncioni and Clarke):

  • Above CP, W′ drains at power − CP joules a second.
  • Below CP, it refills: dW′bal/dt = (W′ − W′bal) × (CP − power) ÷ W′.

Through a rotation the rider's power is their pull power for their own pull and their sitting-in power for the rest, so each rotation drains W′ on the pull and refills some of it in the line. The race starts with W′ full and the low point sinks a little each rotation until drain and refill balance out. The planner counts the rider's pulls in the estimated race time (rounding up), or, without a route, assumes a long race.

For example, a rider with CP {{ w_example.cp }} W and W′ {{ w_example.w_prime_kj }} kJ who pulls {{ w_example.pull_s }} seconds at {{ w_example.front }} W spends {{ w_example.spent_kj }} kJ a pull. If they then sit in for {{ w_example.sit_min }} minutes at {{ w_example.draft }} W, they get back about {{ w_example.refill_pct }}% of whatever is missing each time, and over a long race their W′ balance settles at about {{ w_example.low_kj }} kJ, {{ w_example.low_pct }}% of W′, at the end of each pull.

On the course

Everything in the pull schedule is worked out on level road. When the route has an elevation profile (from the Zwift Speed Lab data the routes page uses), the plan page also shows the team riding it:

  1. The riders' weights and heights are averaged into one typical rider, and that rider's power to hold the target flat speed alone on the flat is worked out.
  2. That power is held steady over the whole route, lead-in included. On each stretch of the profile, about every 50 m, it gives the team's speed there: slower up a climb, faster down a descent. That is the line on the chart. Each stretch's slope is measured over about {{ course_grade_window_m }} m, since a bike's momentum carries it over shorter bumps.
  3. For each rider, the readout gives the power to hold that speed on that stretch: on the front, with no draft, and sitting in, with the plan's average draft saving. A custom pull power scales a rider's pulls in step with the model's own.

Two things stand out on a hilly route. On a climb, everyone's power to hold the team's speed comes out at nearly the same W/kg, so a light rider holds it on fewer watts than a heavy one. And the draft saves a share of the air resistance, which is most of the work on the flat but little of it on a climb, so sitting in there costs nearly as much as pulling. A rider whose sitting-in power goes over their FTP is marked over FTP: they get no rest there.

Move along the profile with the mouse, drag across it on a phone, or focus it and use the arrow keys (Page Up and Page Down jump further, Home and End go to the start and the finish). The chart starts on the steepest stretch.

The steady effort is a model, not a pacing plan: real teams often ease off on climbs so everyone stays together, and push harder on the flat.

Finish time

With an elevation profile, the finish time is the profile ridden at the steady effort described under On the course: each stretch's length over the team's speed on it, added up, lead-in included, since the lead-in is raced. The speed beside it on the Team card is that distance over that time.

When the route has no profile, the planner falls back to a rougher estimate:

  1. The typical rider's power to hold the target flat speed alone on the flat is worked out.
  2. The planner finds the speed that power gives on the route's average gradient: total climbing ÷ distance.
  3. Finish time = distance ÷ that speed.

That treats all the climbing as one steady slope with no descents, so on a hilly course it runs slow, and it leaves the lead-in out.

The race length also sets how many pulls each rider takes, which the W′ balance and TSS depend on.

zwiftgopher comparison

zwiftgopher.com runs its own TTT optimiser. Run optimize sends it your riders (Zwift IDs with the weight, height and FTP the plan uses, or name, weight, height and FTP for guests), your target speed and the upcoming event you choose. Its plan then appears next to ours: finish time, average speed, team power, and a suggested pull order you can copy.

  • Its model is its own, and its route is the upcoming event's, which may not be the one you picked.
  • It takes 2 to 8 riders, and accepts one request a minute, so a run can take about a minute and a half.

What the model leaves out

  • Hills in the pull schedule. Pull powers, IF and the limits are worked out on level road; only the finish time and On the course ride the route's slopes.
  • Pacing. The team rides the course at a steady effort. Easing off on climbs and pushing on the flat is not modelled yet.
  • Equipment. Every rider is on the same TT bike, a TT frame with a deep front wheel and a disc. Ordinary wheels need about 5% more power than the planner shows, and a road bike about 15% more.
  • Where each rider sits. Sitting in uses the average saving over the whole line, not the wheel each rider is actually on at each moment.
  • W′ is an estimate. Zwift Racing's CP and W′ come from each rider's best recent efforts, and the stand-ins for riders without them are rougher still. Real riders also tire over a race in ways the W′ model does not capture.
  • Race dynamics. Surges, corners, power-ups, gaps in the rotation and riders being dropped are not modelled.
  • Data age. Weights, heights and FTPs are copies from ZwiftPower and Zwift Racing, and can be out of date.

Glossary

FTP
Functional threshold power: roughly the power a rider can hold for an hour.
W/kg
Watts per kilogram of body weight.
CdA
Drag area: how much air a rider pushes, in m².
CP
Critical power: the highest power a rider can hold for a long time; close to FTP.
W′
The work a rider can do above CP before they must ease off, in kilojoules.
W′ balance (W′bal)
How much of W′ a rider has left at a given moment.
Pull
A turn on the front of the line.
Rotation
One full turn through every rider who pulls.
NP
Normalized Power: an average that weights hard efforts more, closer to how a varied ride feels.
IF
Intensity factor: NP ÷ FTP.
TSS
Training Stress Score: hours × IF² × 100. An hour at FTP is 100.
